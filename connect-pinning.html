Keep your websites online.

A page you publish is served from your own computer. Most home, office and school networks don't let the web reach a computer, so the link often won't open. A pinning service keeps a copy and serves it for you, even while your computer is off. This takes five minutes, once, and it's free.

Step by step

The service is Filebase. You make an account there, make a folder for your websites, and it gives you a token. You paste that token into Synomy. That's all.

  1. Make a Filebase account.

    Go to filebase.com and click Sign up. Use your email address and a password, or Sign in with Google. If you used email, open the message Filebase sends you and click the link in it.

    You don't need a credit card. The free plan holds 5 GB, and a published page with its pictures is usually well under 1 MB.

  2. Make a bucket.

    A bucket is Filebase's word for a folder. In the Filebase console, click Buckets on the left, then Create Bucket at the top right.

    • Name: small letters, numbers and dashes only, like synomy-sites-anna. It must be a name nobody else on Filebase has used, so add your name or a few numbers.
    • Storage network (if it asks): choose IPFS.
    • Private or public (if it asks): leave it private. Your websites still open for everyone; this only controls who can browse the bucket itself.

    Click Create Bucket.

  3. Make a token for that bucket.

    Click Access Keys on the left (or go to console.filebase.com/keys). Scroll down to the section called IPFS RPC API. Open Choose Bucket to Generate Token, pick the bucket you just made, and copy the Secret Access Token it shows.

    Use the token from the IPFS RPC API section, not the Access Key and Secret Key at the top of the page: those won't work in Synomy. Treat the token like a password: anyone who has it can put files in your bucket. If it ever leaks, rotate it on the same page and paste the new one into Synomy.

  4. Paste it into Synomy.

    In Synomy, click Sites (the globe) in the top bar, then Settings. Under While this computer is off, leave Service on Filebase, paste the token into Access token, and click Connect. Synomy checks the token with Filebase first, so a wrong one is caught right away.

    The token is kept on this computer only. Synomy sends it to Filebase and nowhere else.

  5. That's it.

    Websites you already published go to Filebase straight away, and every page you publish from now on goes there too. In Sites, each website says Pinned at Filebase, and after a minute or two Live. Send the link to anyone: it opens even when your computer is off or asleep.

    The technical bit

    Synomy builds each website on your computer and uploads all of it in one request, as a CAR file, to Filebase's IPFS RPC API (rpc.filebase.io/api/v0/dag/import), which pins it. So Filebase never has to reach your computer, and it works on any network. The token is checked first against Filebase's IPFS Pinning Service API (api.filebase.io/v1/ipfs); Filebase uses the same bucket token for both.

    Republishing a page makes a new version with a new link, and Synomy asks Filebase to let go of the version before it, so an old version doesn't keep taking up room. Pinata and 4EVERLAND are in the same list in Settings. Pinata uploads whole websites only on a paid plan, and 4EVERLAND fetches them from your computer, so on a network that keeps the web out they help less than Filebase does.

Something not working?

Connect says the token was refused. Copy it again from the IPFS RPC API section of Access Keys, without spaces. The Access Key and Secret Key at the top of that page won't work.

A website says "Your pinning service could not pin it". Your free plan may be full. Unpublish the ones you no longer need, or empty old files from the bucket in the Filebase console, then press Republish.

It says Pinned, but the link still shows an error. Press Check now. A gateway can remember a failed try for a few minutes, so give it a moment and open the link again.

What goes to Filebase?

Only the websites you publish: the pages you chose, with their pictures, which anyone with the link can already read. Not your other notes, not your meetings, not your files.

The account, and any bill if you ever outgrow the free plan, are yours. Synomy runs no server and never sees your token. Filebase's own rules are in their privacy policy. You can click Disconnect in Sites › Settings at any time: nothing is sent after that, and you can delete what's already there in the Filebase console.
